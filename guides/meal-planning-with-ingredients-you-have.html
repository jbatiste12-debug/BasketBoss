Practical weekly meal planning

How to plan meals with ingredients you already have

The easiest way to cut duplicate shopping isn't to memorise every cupboard. It's to check what you already have before choosing what to cook and making a shopping list.

BasketBoss guide · Updated 19 November 2026

1. Check your kitchen in five minutes

Before writing next week's shopping list, take a quick look in the fridge, freezer and cupboards. Start with ingredients that are likely to be forgotten: half-used cheese, vegetables, opened packs, frozen portions, rice, pasta and tins.

You don't need an exact inventory. A short note of what is available, running low or out is enough to stop many repeat purchases. For anything you plan to rely on, check the amount you actually have.

2. Pick meals around what needs using

Choose two or three ingredients you already own and build meals around them. For example, leftover broccoli, a tin of chickpeas and some rice could prompt a curry or a quick stir-fry, depending on what else is available.

Prioritise food that needs using sooner, but check storage conditions and use-by dates. A meal plan is never a reason to use unsafe food.

3. Write a list for the gaps, not the whole recipe

Once you've selected the meals, compare their ingredients against what is at home. If the recipe needs two tins of tomatoes and you already have one, add only the missing tin to the shopping list. If you've got enough rice, don't add another bag by habit.

This also works for household essentials. A quick check of toilet rolls, washing-up liquid and cleaning spray can prevent the familiar second trip to the shop.

4. Update the pantry as you shop and cook

After shopping, confirm what you actually bought. After cooking, update ingredients you actually used. Neither action should be assumed from a plan alone: people change recipes, substitute ingredients and sometimes put things back on the shelf.

Before the next weekly shop, check only the ingredients needed for upcoming meals or items you're unsure about. That is usually more practical than counting everything in the kitchen all over again.

Common questions

Do I need to record every ingredient?

No. Start with the food relevant to the meals you plan, especially opened packs and perishable ingredients. Add detail when it helps you avoid a duplicate purchase.

What if my plans change during the week?

Review what you actually cooked and bought. A useful pantry record should be easy to correct, rather than assuming your meal plan always happened exactly as scheduled.

Will this automatically save money?

It can help reduce duplicate purchases and avoidable waste, but actual savings depend on your household, prices and shopping habits.
