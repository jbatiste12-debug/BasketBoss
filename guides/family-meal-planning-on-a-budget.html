How I plan family meals around a £100 weekly shop

A UK-focused, practical method for planning dinners before you spend — not a promise that every household can feed everyone for £100.

From the BasketBoss founder · Firefighter and dad of four

I’m a firefighter and a dad of four. Between shifts, school schedules and family life, our food shops could easily pass £150 when we walked in without a proper plan. I built BasketBoss because I wanted a calmer way to decide what we were eating and what needed to go into the trolley. A £100 weekly food shop can be a useful planning target, but it is not a guarantee: actual prices and needs depend on who is eating, where you shop, dietary needs and what is already in your kitchen.

The goal is not a perfect spreadsheet or tiny portions. It is a clear starting plan you can adjust, with meals your family might actually eat.

1. Start with what is already in the kitchen

Before writing a shopping list, check the fridge, freezer and cupboards. Note the usable quantities of meat, vegetables, rice, pasta, tins and dairy, along with use-by dates. Build one or two dinners around the ingredients that need using first. Do not count food that has spoiled or stock you have not measured. If you already have pasta, a sauce and some frozen vegetables, your dinner plan has a head start.

I keep this check short enough to do after a shift. Ten minutes can reveal duplicate purchases before they become another half-used pack in the fridge.

2. Choose five dinners, not fourteen elaborate recipes

Plan five realistic evening meals. Leave room for leftovers, flexible lunches or one easy freezer meal. Choose a mix of a traybake, a pasta dish, a vegetarian dinner and family favourites. Reuse ingredients sensibly: peppers bought for fajitas might also work in a pasta bake. A shared ingredient is helpful only if its pack size fits the amount you will genuinely use.

Write the servings beside every dinner. A busy Tuesday might need four portions; a full family Sunday might need more. Making those decisions before shopping is easier than discovering a shortfall while cooking.

3. Give the £100 target a flexible basket plan

As an illustrative split, I might reserve £22 for protein, £23 for fruit and vegetables, £19 for rice, pasta, bread and other staples, £13 for dairy and alternatives, £11 for cupboard items and £12 as a buffer. That adds up to £100, but these are planning envelopes, not supermarket price quotations. Your household may need a very different split, especially for specialist foods, breakfasts, packed lunches or large appetites.

Price the actual basket at the shop you intend to use. If the estimates total £117, change one or two recipes or use an ingredient already in stock; do not pretend the basket costs £100. Keep a buffer for essentials and inevitable changes.

4. Turn recipes into one list by aisle

Merge the ingredients from your planned dinners, then group them under produce, meat and fish, dairy, bakery and cupboard. Check the pack sizes against recipe quantities. For example, three meals might use one bag of onions rather than three separate bags. A tidy list reduces backtracking, but always confirm unit prices, weights and availability in store.

BasketBoss helps me build meal plans and organise the resulting shopping list by aisle. Its estimates are guidance, not live retailer quotes, so I still check the shelf labels before paying.

5. Review the week without feeling you failed

At the end of the week, look at what was actually cooked, what was wasted and whether top-up trips pushed the budget higher. If a meal was unpopular, replace it next time. If you always need an easy Friday dinner, plan one. The routine should fit real life, including overtime, children changing their minds and the occasional takeaway.

Small improvements matter more than an unrealistic target. For me, successful planning means fewer last-minute decisions and a clear idea of what food is already available.

Frequently asked questions

Can a family really eat for £100 a week in the UK?

Some households may manage it, but £100 is a flexible example, not a guaranteed cost. Family size, location, dietary needs and what is already in the kitchen all change the total.

How many dinners should I plan before shopping?

Five planned dinners plus flexible leftover or freezer nights is a practical starting point. Adjust the number to your actual schedule.

Does BasketBoss know live supermarket prices?

No. BasketBoss uses indicative estimates. Always check current retailer prices and pack sizes before buying.
